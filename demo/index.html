LARR: Language-Model-Attenuated Recognition Reward
and Localized Credit Assignment
for TTS Reinforcement Learning

Rui Song1, Yajun Hu2, Chang Liu2, Lei Sun2, Xin Fang2, Jianqing Gao2, Guoping Hu2

1University of Science and Technology of China    2iFLYTEK Research

Abstract

Text-to-speech (TTS) reinforcement learning often uses utterance-level rewards from text ASR to improve pronunciation. Yet contextual ASR can mask a pronunciation error by exploiting linguistic context, hiding it from the transcript. In our experiments, contextual ASR detects only 19.6–31.3% of pronunciation errors. Moreover, such a reward cannot identify which acoustic tokens caused the error. This leaves TTS reinforcement learning without feedback that is both sensitive to phone errors and localized in time. We propose Language-Model-Attenuated Recognition Reward (LARR) and a phone-level credit-assignment scheme to address these limitations. LARR uses a phone recognizer trained with history-token replacement to reduce contextual repair, while dense LARR assigns credit to individual phones, thereby targeting individual pronunciation errors. We apply GRPO fine-tuning to a pre-trained LLM-based TTS model in both zero-shot and single-speaker SFT-to-RL settings. Experimental results show that the proposed method substantially improves both the intelligibility and naturalness of synthesized speech.

Dense LARR: Phone-Aligned Credit Assignment

This compact view highlights how Dense LARR moves recognition feedback from a phone position to the corresponding TTS token span. The frame-level phone logits used for span mapping may come from either a frame-level phoneme recognizer or a CTC phoneme recognizer; the logits are assumed to be available. Architecture details and training hyperparameters are omitted.

1
Phone hypothesis
Recognize phones from each rollout waveform.
2
Error labels
Align the hypothesis with the reference and mark affected target phones.
3
Span mapping
Use constrained monotonic phone spans from the frame-level logits, then transfer them through the edit path to TTS policy-token spans.
4
Dense credit
Normalize phone-position labels across same-prompt rollouts and apply them to mapped spans.
Compact pseudocode
for each rollout i:
    H_i ← phone_recognizer(acoustic_codes(W_i))
    E_i ← levenshtein_backtrace(H_i, G_i)
    q_i,m ← −1 if E_i marks m (substitution/deletion;
             insertion marks its adjacent target phone(s))
             +1 otherwise

    S_i^H ← constrained_phone_spans(phone_logits_i, phone_order=H_i)
    S_i^G ← transfer_hypothesis_spans(S_i^H, E_i)
    φ_i ← map_ctc_steps_to_policy_tokens(S_i^G, policy_token_positions_i)

for each target-phone position m in the same-prompt group:
    a_i,m ← standardize_across_rollouts({q_j,m})
    A_i,t ← mask_i,t · a_i,m  if φ_i(t) = m, else 0

if phone/edit/token alignment fails: fall back to scalar GRPO

The key distinction is span-level localization: tokens outside an aligned phone span receive no dense phone credit. The alignment interface is agnostic to whether the logits come from a frame-level or CTC phone recognizer.
